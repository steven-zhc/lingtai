## Design note: #951, the report carries the spend

### Where it goes

Two files carry the change. The rest is markup and CSS.

- `packages/conductor/src/report.ts`: `Report` gains two fields:
  - `costliest: TopTicket[]`
  - `spend.byPurpose: { purpose: string; money: Money }[]`
- `apps/board/src/app/report/`:
  - `page.tsx` reads today's spend.
  - `Tiles` in `panels.tsx` grows to eight tiles.
  - Two new panel components draw *where it went* and *the costliest tickets*.
- CSS goes in `apps/board/src/app/globals.css`, beside the existing `.rp-tile` rules. Take the class names and rules from the mock: `rp-tiles four`, `sp-today`, `mtr`, `hot`, `sw-bar`/`sw-seg`/`sw-rows`, and `tc`.

### `costliest` is its own sort, not `ranked`

Do not compute `costliest` from `ranked`. `ranked` follows `input.sortBy` (`report.ts:485-494`), and `sortBy` is the `?sort=` the *went round most* panel toggles (`panels.tsx:194-201`, `page.tsx:81`). If `costliest` were `ranked` sliced, the costliest panel would reorder by rounds whenever that toggle is on rounds.

Write a second sort over the same `placed` list:
1. `ordering(ticketMoney(t))` descending
2. then `fixRounds` descending
3. then `ticket` ascending

Take `.slice(0, 8).map(topTicket)`. `top` and the toggle stay as they are.

A ticket whose cost is entirely unknown has an `ordering` of 0 (`report.ts:239`), so it sorts last. That is the right place, because nothing known says it was expensive. Its row must still print `n unknown` and never `$0.00`. `moneyText` already does that (`lib/report.ts:218-224`).

### `spend.byPurpose` sums the days, it does not recompute

Build it by adding up `days[].byPurpose` purpose by purpose with `addMoney`. Do **not** run `spendMoney` over the window's runs grouped by purpose:
- `splitByPurpose` shares each day's single rounded estimate out by largest remainder, so that "the parts add up to the day" (`report.ts:276-279`).
- Summing those parts therefore adds up exactly to `spend.total`.
- Recomputing per purpose over the window rounds each purpose on its own, so *where it went* could disagree with the *spent in the window* tile by a cent.

Unit test: the sum of `byPurpose` equals `spend.total` field by field. Use estimates that would round differently if recomputed.

Keep the purposes as `spendPurpose` names them (`Work`, `Answering`, `Judge`, `Triage`, `Discussion`; `report.ts:207-208`), sorted the way `splitByPurpose` sorts them. Grouping is the board's job:
- The board folds `Triage` and `Discussion` into one row through `purposeSeriesOf` and `PURPOSE_SERIES` (`lib/report.ts:277-288`).
- That gives the mock's "triage and discussion" and the same colours as the *spend per day* legend.
- Rows and bar segments follow `PURPOSE_SERIES` order, as the mock draws them.

### The shares

Every share on the page has the same denominator: `centsOf(spend.total)`, reported plus estimated (`lib/report.ts:226`).
- The *answering refusals* tile is the `Answering` entry's `centsOf` divided by that denominator. Each row of *where it went* is its own entry's `centsOf` divided by the same.
- Unknown runs are in neither the numerator nor the denominator. Where a purpose has `unknown > 0`, its row says `+ n unknown`, and the tile's sub-line says so too.
- A denominator of 0 makes the tile `—` (`percent(null)`, `lib/report.ts:234`), and the bar says *nothing spent in the window*.
- A window with only unknown runs is also *nothing known spent*, plus the unknown count. It is never `0%`.

### The tiles

- **The order:**
  - Row one: landed, median fix rounds, five rounds or more, landed in 0–1 rounds.
  - Row two: spent in the window, spent today, answering refusals, median machine time.
  - *Median machine time* moves from row one to the end of row two; its content is unchanged.
- **The per-ticket figure is the median, not what the mock prints.** The mock's sub-line says `$5.35 per landed ticket`. That is `$920.57 ÷ 172`, total divided by landed, which the ticket's Watch out forbids. Print `median $X per landed ticket` from `landed.cost.medianCents` (`report.ts:82-83`):
  - prefix `~` when `medianIncludesEstimate`;
  - add `+ n unknown` when `landed.cost.unknown > 0`;
  - leave the clause out when `medianCents` is null.
  - The window's own `· n unknown`, at `panels.tsx:73`, stays. The mock dropped it.
- **Spent today:**
  - `Tiles` takes a `DaySpendReading` beside the report slice.
  - `day === null`: the value is *could not be read* and the title is `reading.text`. Never a figure.
  - `ceilingUsd === undefined`: no meter and no `of $…`.
  - When `query.project` is set, the tile's title says the figure is the installation's, since the ceiling covers every project (`day-spend.ts:2-4`).
- **One meter, not two copies.** The meter arithmetic is in `Footer` (`footer.tsx:58-79`): shares capped at the ceiling, `role="meter"`, and `aria-valuetext={day.text}`. Lift it into an exported pure component in `footer.tsx` and use it from both places.
  - `footer.tsx` must stay free of server imports, because "#906 mounts it in a client component" (`footer.tsx:7-8`). The lifted component imports only React and types, which keeps that true.
  - The footer on `/report` will also show today's figure. That duplication is accepted, because the footer is out of scope.

### The page

- Call `readDayReading()` in `page.tsx`, outside the `try` that builds `report`, and run it concurrently with `currentReport`.
  - It never throws (`day-spend.ts:36-51`).
  - Keeping it out of that `try` means a failed report read still leaves the day's figure, and a failed day read never blanks the report.
- **Effect ratchet:** this adds no `async` function and no `Effect.run*`. `ReportPage` is already async, and `readDayReading` already exists. Do not wrap it in a new async helper. `packages/domain/unit/effect-ratchet.test.ts` would count one, and the baseline is never raised.

### The panels

- Both go inside `rp-grid`, right after *spend per day* and before the wide *why work stopped to wait* (`page.tsx:126-138`). The grid's two columns pair them without new layout.
- **The costliest tickets.** One row per ticket, as the mock draws it: ticket link, kind, rounds, a reported/estimated bar scaled to the largest ticket in the list, `moneyText(cost)`, and `purposeShares[0]` in `purposeWords`.
  - The mock's subtitle says *landed in the window*, but the ticket says every outcome, and the ticket wins. Write *placed in the window, any outcome*.
  - On any row whose `outcome !== 'landed'`, show the outcome.
- **Where it went.** One bar, with a solid reported segment and a pale estimated segment per series. Each row shows the amount, the share, and `$… reported · ~$… est.`, plus `+ n unknown` where there is any.

### Tests

- `packages/conductor/unit/report.test.ts`:
  - `costliest` stays the same under `sortBy: 'rounds'` and `sortBy: 'cost'`.
  - It includes a closed and an open ticket.
  - An all-unknown ticket sorts last.
  - `byPurpose` sums to `total`, and its unknown counts carry through.
- `apps/board/unit/report-panels.test.tsx`, over the pure components:
  - spent today with `day: null`, with no ceiling, and with a project filter;
  - the share tile at a zero denominator;
  - no `$0.00` where only unknown cost exists;
  - a non-landed row shows its outcome.
- No whole-page snapshot.

What none of these checks is the server/client import boundary of the lifted meter. Only the Next build sees that (`apps/release/integration/build.test.ts`), and it runs after the merge. The unit-level stand-in is to keep `footer.tsx`'s imports to React, `next/link` and types; a reviewer can check that by reading the file's import lines.
