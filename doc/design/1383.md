## The decision the ticket leaves open: what kind of text the chapter is

**The chapter makes no `file.ts:line` citations and gives no dated anecdotes.** The guide's three sections are written for a Lingtai developer. They say `prompts/ticket.md:5` is `{{body}}`, but it is now at `prompts/ticket.md:90`, which shows how quickly line citations go stale. They also cite `conduct.ts:3929`, `discover.ts:205/219` and `prompt.ts:400`. ADR 0112 §16 says *"A chapter says what Lingtai is and what to do, not why it was designed so"*, and §15 says *"Each fact has one home."* So the implementing agent must check each surviving claim against the code before writing it, but the chapter states the claim as plain fact. The current lines are:

- The body is the prompt: `prompts/ticket.md:90` `{{body}}`.
- Commit each piece: `packages/conductor/src/prompt.ts:400`, *"Commit each piece that works as you finish it … An attempt that is cut off is redone from the head"*.
- No kind label means invisible: `discover.ts:276` `skip: 'no-kind'`.
- Open blockers pass a ticket over: `discover.ts:290` `blocked-by`.

**Which sentences survive, by the ticket's (a)/(b)/(c) rule:**

- **Keep two numbers, because each one makes a point.** First, #317 versus #308: $0.92 for 27 turns against $17.24 for 239 turns, with Opus to Sonnet worth only about 2.19× per turn, so ticket size is the bigger lever. Second, a split happens **once**, into up to 8 children, and the parent's *Done when* is the contract.
- **Drop** the #811 anecdote (2026-11-01). Keep its lesson as one line under *Out of scope*.
- **(a) Link, do not restate:**
  - `lingtai run … --no-merge`, `lingtai ask`/`answer` and `lingtai now` → `cli.md#lingtai-run`, `#lingtai-ask` and `#lingtai-now`. Each already exists (`cli.md:106`, `:414`, `:132`).
  - Kinds as vocabulary, filter and priority; `exclude` as the thing that makes a hold label work; open blockers only → `plugins/queue.md`. It already says all three (`queue.md:22-26`, `:43`).
  - The split's bound → `reference.md:400` (`ticketSplits`). That page is off the reading path, so link to it once at most, or not at all.
- **(b)** Nothing here lacks a reference home, so nothing moves into `cli.md`, `plugins/` or `requirements.md`.
- **(c)** Drop *"Since #643 a fix of a refusal and the cold reviewer read it too"* and *"guessing a kind would put unclassified issues at the front"*. Both are reasons, not instructions.

**The four-row table is one example, never the rule.** The guide's *Do this* list (`:27-41`) presents Problem/Want/Fix/Watch out as the prescribed opening. In the chapter, the rule is what a body must settle for the agent: what is wrong, what done looks like, the trap, the evidence, the edges, and what is out of scope. *This repository opens every ticket with a four-row table* is at most one sentence offered as an example (ticket's *Watch out*).

**Blocked by covers both ticket sources.** The guide's `:77-78` says *"These rules are about GitHub issues"*. The tutorial, however, creates local tickets with `lingtai ticket new` (`doc/tutorial.md:41`), which has `--blocked-by` (`cli.md:273-277`) and `--unheld` (`cli.md:215`). The chapter's *order* section says a chain goes in the ticket's own *blocked by* (GitHub's dependency on an issue, or the `blocked by:` line on a local ticket) and never in the body. Link to `cli.md#lingtai-ticket` for the flag. Do not describe the board's form: it is out of scope.

**Shape.** Use four `##` sections in the order the ticket's *Want* gives: *Write the body as the prompt*, *Size it for one attempt*, *Order it with kinds and blocked by*, *Hold what is not ready*. The fifth and last section is `## Next` with one link, `[When something fails](when-it-fails.md)`, written the same way as `board.md:62-64`. Keep fenced blocks to a minimum. A sample ticket skeleton is allowed and does not count toward the word limit, but the prose has to carry the rules.
