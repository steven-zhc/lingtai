# #314 — the shape of the change

## Where the group goes

`packages/recipe/src/recipe.ts` already has the pattern for a field group two plugins share: `SOURCE_FIELDS` (`recipe.ts:901`) and `QUEUE_FIELDS` (`recipe.ts:976`), both `satisfies PluginFields`, both spread into a plugin's `fields`. A `DISPATCH_FIELDS` beside them, spread into `agentPlugin.fields` (`recipe.ts:186`) and `judgePlugin.fields` (`recipe.ts:1194`), is the whole of §3. `PluginFields` is `Readonly<Record<string, z.ZodType>>` (`plugin.ts:145`) and nothing in `definePlugin` cares where a field came from, so a spread group is a group.

Three things about the declarations that are not style:

- **`model` and `limits` are `.optional()` with no `.default()`.** `resolveSource` hashes the *resolved* recipe — *"The resolved form is what gets hashed"* (`resolve.ts:246`) — so a `limits: z.object({…}).default({})` would put a new key in every resolved recipe and change every `configHash` on both machines. `runtime.assignee` is the precedent and says so at `recipe.ts:2812`: *"Optional rather than defaulted so that a recipe that says nothing hashes and emits exactly as it did."* This is also what makes 0070 §9's *a migration of exactly nothing* true.
- **`wall` gets its own `positiveDuration` refine, where it is written.** Three schemas hold a duration and all three check it at the key: `BACKOFF` (`recipe.ts:900`), `runtime.limits.wall` (`recipe.ts:2723`), `MachineLimits.wall` (`local.ts:60`), and the third one's comment is the reason — `wall: "90"` *"resolved cleanly and threw later inside the board's reading of it, where it was read as the recipe could not be read (#218)"*. A fourth place a duration is written is a fourth `.refine`.
- **`limits` is a `strictObject` of `turns` and `wall` only.** `rounds`/`restarts` written inside a dispatch are then refused by zod as unknown keys, which is Done-when 4 for free — but the message zod gives for an unrecognised key is worse than the sentence `judgePlugin`'s own docblock already promises (`recipe.ts:1183`: *"a recipe writing either under a judge is refused by name, listing what this plugin does declare"*). Name them explicitly in the refusal; `rounds` under a judge is the loop-that-only-spends failure that docblock is about, and it deserves the sentence rather than `Unrecognized key`.

`judge:` takes no `prompt:` — 0070's *What this does not decide* leaves it out, and `judgePrompt` (`judge-agent.ts:86`) stays fixed.

## The narrowing refusal: a top-level refinement, not `actionsAt`

The obvious home is wrong. The per-step refusals live in `actionsAt(step)`'s `superRefine` (`recipe.ts:2288` is the newest), and a refinement inside `steps:` cannot see `runtime.limits` — it is a sibling branch. So the check goes on the `Recipe` object itself, after its shape, walking `steps × actions` against `data.runtime.limits`.

Two facts that make that safe, both checked in this worktree:

- **Zod 4.5 keeps `.shape` and `ZodObject`-ness through `.superRefine`.** `unknownKeys` reads `Recipe.shape` (`resolve.ts:286`) and `LIMIT_DEFAULTS` reads `Recipe.shape.runtime.shape.limits` (`recipe.ts:2863`); neither breaks. (`z.object({…}).superRefine(() => {})` → `shape: object`, `instanceof z.ZodObject: true`.)
- **`runtime.limits` is present by then even though the recipe file may not carry it.** `resolveLocalRecipe`'s `shape` callback writes `raw["runtime"] = {…, agent, limits}` before `Recipe.safeParse` (`local.ts:415`), and refuses the key written in the recipe itself two lines up (`local.ts:406`). So the refinement compares the recipe's own `steps:` against the machine file's ceiling, already merged.

That second fact is the non-obvious consequence: **the two sides of this refusal live in different files.** The ceiling is `~/.lingtai/config.yml`'s (0046 §3), the narrowing is `~/.lingtai/<project>/recipe.yml`'s. The refusal must therefore name the key and say whose file it is, because *raise `runtime.limits.turns`* sends a person to a line the recipe is not allowed to have — `local.ts:406`'s own refusal already does this well and is the wording to copy. Provenance (`runtime.limits.turns: 150 ← ~/.lingtai/config.yml`, built at `local.ts:365`) is **not** available inside a schema refinement; it is attached after `resolveSource` returns. Do not restructure to get it — name the key, not the path.

Where a step's own entry failed to resolve, the check does not run, exactly as the lane rule does not (`recipe.ts:2321`: *"Asked only where every entry resolved"*). That is the existing shape, not a new caveat.

## `limitsFor` is the function this ticket finally makes read its step

`settings.ts:65` is the seam, and its `void step;` is the placeholder:

> **The `step` argument is not read, and that is the point.** A function that took no step would have to grow one later, which is the sixty-file change this exists to avoid.

Two decisions it needs, and neither is in the ticket.

**1. A step may hold several dispatches, so `limitsFor(recipe, step)` answers the step's *upper* bound and never a dispatch's own.** `StepMap` refuses a second `worktree:`, `queue:` or lane at a step, but two `agent:` entries at `review` are legal. So there are two questions with two readers, and conflating them is how a reading ends up under-reporting what a pass may spend:

- *what may this **call** spend* → the action's own `limits:`, read at the seam that builds the dispatch. Never `limitsFor`.
- *what may this **step** spend at most* → `limitsFor(recipe, step)`: the **maximum** over the step's dispatches, and `runtime.limits` where none narrows. This is what `passCeiling`'s sentence is made of.

Return the ceiling **object itself** when nothing at the step narrows. `settings.test.ts:71` asserts `toBe(RECIPE.runtime.limits)` — identity — and keeping that true is worth more than it costs: it is the cheap proof that a recipe nobody edited resolves to the same values.

**2. Two of the existing callers are writers and must not be narrowed.** `local.ts:505` and `apps/board/src/app/setup/wizard/finish.ts:118` both do `limits: limitsFor(recipe, "implement")` to write the **machine file's `runtime.limits`** — the ceiling. Narrow those and the wizard silently lowers the ceiling to whatever `implement` asked for, on the next save, permanently. `wizard-page.ts:384` and `:753` are the same question (the dials edit the machine file) and `doctor.ts:1602`'s `runtime: <project> limits` row prints the ceiling beside its provenance.

**And two of the readers mean the ceiling as well**, which the list of writers above misses: `doctor.ts:1602`'s row is one, and `apps/board/src/lib/queued.ts:265` is the other — it feeds `planOf`, whose `PlanView.turns` and `.wall` are documented as `runtime.limits.turns` and `runtime.limits.wall` and whose footer (`plan.tsx`) promises *`runtime.limits` in the recipe's own words … so the line reads back against the file it came from*. Narrow that one and the Queued panel prints `implement`'s reduction under a field documented as the ceiling: two numbers for one documented field, and the next reader comparing the panel against `~/.lingtai/config.yml` goes looking for a bug in the machine-file merge.

`pnpm typecheck` cannot catch a mix-up: both questions return `Recipe["runtime"]["limits"]`. So add a second accessor — `ceilingOf(recipe)` — in `settings.ts`, and go through the eleven call sites by hand naming the question each is asking. It has to be an accessor and cannot be an inlined `recipe.runtime.limits`: `settings.test.ts:327`'s *nothing reaches past them* greps every `src/` line in the workspace for `\w\.runtime\.limits` and fails on it (`MOVING`, `settings.test.ts:326`).

**3. `settings.test.ts:281` is a test this ticket deletes.** Its name is *"answer the same for every step, because the settings have not moved yet"* and its body is `expect(limitsFor(RECIPE, "build")).toEqual(limitsFor(RECIPE, "implement"))`. Replace it with its opposite, on a recipe where `implement` narrows — that assertion is the unit-level proof of the whole feature.

## The dispatch seam: add a factory, do not invert the port

`apps/cli/src/run.ts:233` already argued against the obvious move and its argument still holds:

> The alternative — inverting `RunOnceOptions.runtime` into a factory the conductor calls after resolving — would change a port every test that supplies a fake runtime passes through, to save that one read.

So keep `RunOnceOptions.runtime` as *the pass's default* — what `runtime.agent` named, constructed at `apps/cli/src/conduct.ts:135` and `run.ts:254` — and **add** one optional field beside it: a `(id: RuntimeId) => Runtime`, defaulting to `createRuntime` from `packages/agent/src/runtimes.ts`. Every existing test keeps passing `runtime:` alone and behaves as today; the two-runtime test supplies a map of fakes. 0070 §1 parks the `RuntimeId → factory` table as *"belongs to whichever ticket makes a second runtime actually dispatch"* — it was built by #313 and that paragraph is stale: `RUNTIMES` is `Record<RuntimeId, …>` so *"a `RuntimeId` is dispatchable now because it has a row here and for no other reason"* (`runtimes.ts:9`). Nothing new is needed. (`propose.ts:347`'s `runtime ?? "claude-code"` is the last hand-written name and is out of scope.)

Three dispatch sites read one `const limits = limitsFor(recipe, "implement")` (`conduct.ts:558`) today, at three different steps. Each becomes its own question:

- **`implement`** — `dispatch`'s `spend` (`conduct.ts:2352`) and the runtime at `conduct.ts:2423`/`:2354`. The spec handed through `stepDeps.work` (`conduct.ts:2023`) carries `prompt` and `model` already; it gains the action's `limits` and its `agent`. A fix round is this same `dispatch` again, so a narrowed `implement` bounds the rounds too — which is what makes the ticket's *"then 3 round(s) at implement's bound"* arithmetic true rather than a wording.
- **the cold reviewer** — `from-recipe.ts:196`. Its comment *"`agent` itself is **not** passed on, and that is not it being dropped"* (`from-recipe.ts:201`) is the line that stops being true; this is where the runtime is chosen now. **Do not `{...deps.agent}`**: `stepDeps.agent.settingsPath` is a getter on purpose (`conduct.ts:1988`) — *"read when the action runs … so always after `cut`"* — and a spread evaluates it at build time. Write the five keys out with `settingsPath` re-declared as a getter delegating to `deps.agent`; a sixth key added to `AgentActionDeps` later is then a compile error here, which is the right place for it.
- **the judge** — `askTheAgent` (`conduct.ts:1617`), whose `limits: { turns: limits.turns, wallMs: … }` at `conduct.ts:1685` is the pass ceiling and whose `run` request carries no `model` at all. The model and limits reach it on `Declared` (`judge.ts:138`), filled by `judgeDeclaredAt` off the action — **not on `Judging`**. `pass-steps.ts:360`'s docblock is explicit: `Object.keys` of a judge's brief must be exactly `evidence, findings, offering, when`, and *"a field added to `Judging` fails there by name, which is the whole reason that assertion is a key set"*. A dispatch's price is not part of the question a judge is asked.

`RuntimeCapabilities.enforces` and `missingForTier` are unchanged: the tier stays per-pass (0070 §8), and `missingForTier(options.runtime.capabilities, tier)` at `conduct.ts:622` is now a question about **each** runtime a step names, not only the default — a `sandboxed` recipe with a step naming a runtime that cannot provide it must still record `DispatchRefused` before the claim rather than at the step.

## What the log has to keep saying

`RunStarted.runtime` is written from `options.runtime.capabilities.id` (`conduct.ts:2384`). With a step dispatching something else that field becomes a lie, and it is the field that answers *which runtime wrote this*. Write the **dispatched** runtime. No schema change, no upcaster — the field exists and `model` beside it already carries `spec.model ?? ""`.

The judge has no event of its own, by decision: *"What it cost is in the sentence rather than on an event of its own"* (`conduct.ts:1613`). So a judge that names a model records it nowhere unless the sentence says so — put the model in `runLog.note`/`PassRouted.why` beside the runtime at `conduct.ts:1640`. A cheap judge whose whole point is the model is one whose model has to be readable afterwards.

## `passCeiling`, and the one line

`passCeiling` (`ceiling.ts:36`) takes a flat `{rounds, restarts, turns, wall, wallMs}` and has five callers, two of which have no recipe at all: `wizard-page.ts:566` (the dials, live in the browser) and `apps/cli/src/wall-limit.ts:24` (a module constant off `LIMIT_DEFAULTS`). Do not add a parameter they must pass. Add an **optional field to the object** — the steps that narrow, as `{step, turns, wall, wallMs}` — filled by a new `settings.ts` accessor and carried on `ProjectFilter.limits` (`filter.ts:207`). That one addition reaches `lingtai status` (`filter.ts:280`), the board's chip (`board.ts:628`), `lingtai doctor` (`doctor.ts:1535`) and `lingtai add` (`onboard.ts:326`) without any of them changing, which is what `ceiling.ts`'s header asks for: *"the same sentence `lingtai status` and `lingtai add` print, called rather than rewritten"*.

**Keep the arithmetic and name only the steps that narrow.** Today's sentence counts *agent runs* — the work plus `rounds` back to it — and not every dispatching step; it never included the cold reviewer's own wall. Making it a true sum over all dispatches is a different change, it would alter the printed number for every recipe on both machines, and Done-when 7 says a recipe that declares none of this behaves exactly as today. So: a recipe that narrows nothing must print today's string character for character (pin that with a test against `LIMIT_DEFAULTS`), and a step that narrows appears as its own figure with the total still `implement`'s bound × (rounds + 1). That is the reading the ticket's sketch shows, and it keeps one line.

## `agentRefusal` changes subject, and what it can honestly ask

`conduct.ts:272`'s loop stops refusing a runtime that is merely not the default. What must not go with it is the refusal 0046 §3 is about, and here the ticket and 0070 §7 need a decision the code does not currently make anywhere: **nothing checks that the named runtime is signed in before a pass.** `resolveAgent` (`local.ts:197`) asks `signedIn` *only* when no file names an agent — *"absent, none → refuse by name"* — so a recipe that names `codex` on a machine signed into neither resolves today and fails at spawn.

Use the probe that exists: `signedInHere` (`projects.ts:136`), which is `signedInProbe(everyRuntime())` and caches for 60 seconds precisely because *"Each probe is a process, and the board resolves the recipe on every render"*. Ask it once in `conduct.ts`, beside the tier check at `conduct.ts:622` and before the claim, for the set of runtimes this pass will actually need — `runtime.agent` plus every `agent:`/non-built-in `judge:` in the file. Keep `agentRefusal`'s shape (`AgentRefusal.at`/`.key` exist so a remedy can name the line and the key, `conduct.ts:240`), change its second argument from `dispatched: string` to the set, and rewrite the sentence at `conduct.ts:553` — *"per-step dispatch is not built, so one runtime runs a whole pass"* is the clause being deleted, and `conduct.ts:266`'s *"Per-step dispatch is not built; until it is, the only honest answer … is to say so"* is the docblock being cashed in.

A runtime *nothing* answers for is a refusal before the claim. It is not a `DispatchRefused` about a tier: keep `stage: "recipe"` and the existing return, because the recipe is what names something this machine cannot run.

## What can be proved before the merge

All of it, in `pnpm test`. The two-runtime pass is a unit test: `packages/conductor/test/one-pass.ts:377` exports a fake `Runtime` whose `capabilities.id` is `"claude-code"`, and the pass tests inject it as `runtime:` (`conduct-a-whole-pass.test.ts:69` and fifteen others). A second fake with `id: "codex"` and a `run` that records who was asked, handed in through the new factory field, is the whole apparatus — `capabilities` must stay the real shape for the reason that file already gives: *"`missingForTier` reads them and refuses the dispatch"*. Nothing here needs Postgres, a process or the network, so nothing here belongs in the 803-second half.

The claims worth a named test, each cheap:

- a pass whose `review` names the other runtime dispatches it, and `RunStarted.runtime` on the implement run still says the first (Done-when 1, and the log half of it);
- `limitsFor(recipe, "build") !== limitsFor(recipe, "implement")` when `implement` narrows, and `toBe(recipe.runtime.limits)` when nothing does;
- `Recipe.safeParse` refuses a widening `limits:`, by the sentence, and refuses `rounds:` inside one;
- `passCeiling(LIMIT_DEFAULTS-shaped input)` is byte-identical to today's string.

`doc/plugins/agent.md` and `doc/plugins/judge.md` exist and document these plugins' keys; both gain the group. `plugin-pages.test.ts` judges sections and rows, not fields, so it will not fail on a page left stale — which is why updating them is part of this and not #312's six.

0070 is accepted and this builds it. Nothing here contradicts it, so there is no superseding file; the only stale sentence found is §1's *two places do compare a runtime by hand*, which #313 already fixed, and saying so belongs in this ticket's commit message rather than in an edit to the ADR.
